# CHAPTER 4 — FOOD SAFETY & HYGIENE
## سلامة وصحة الأغذية

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** this chapter's inspection domain (food-contact surfaces, cold-chain control, worker hygiene, pest exclusion) directly governs the primary transmission pathways for foodborne pathogens — the highest-consequence, most time-sensitive risk category in this manual after Chapter 3's life-safety findings.
- **Environmental impact:** secondary — food-processing waste streams are governed primarily by Chapter 2, though this chapter's pest-control criteria overlap structurally with Chapter 2/Chapter 8.
- **Administrative importance:** Algeria's core food-hygiene instrument (Décret 17-140) is unusual among the instruments reviewed in this manual in that it directly and explicitly mandates HACCP-based procedures **by law**, not merely by technical guideline — this gives HACCP-related inspection criteria a firmer legal basis than most other "international best practice" items referenced elsewhere in this manual.
- **Safety implications:** minimal direct overlap with Chapter 3, except where food-processing equipment introduces its own ignition sources (ovens, fryers — cross-reference as needed per facility type).

## 3. Scientific Background

Food-hygiene inspection targets the same small set of transmission pathways in every jurisdiction: temperature abuse (allowing pathogen growth in the "danger zone" between refrigeration and cooking temperatures), cross-contamination (raw-to-ready-to-eat, or via unwashed hands/surfaces), and vector introduction (pests carrying pathogens into food-contact areas). Décret 17-140's HACCP mandate operationalizes control of all three by requiring the establishment to identify its own critical control points rather than relying solely on a generic external checklist — meaning a well-implemented facility-specific HACCP plan is, in principle, a stronger control than a fixed inspection checklist alone. The inspector's role is therefore twofold: verify the generic hygiene floor (Section 6) AND verify the facility's own HACCP plan actually exists and identifies points specific to its own process.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** cold-storage equipment and any visible temperature display; food-contact surface condition (smooth, non-porous, visibly clean); worker PPE and handwashing-station presence/use; visible pest activity or exclusion measures (screens, traps); raw/ready-to-eat product separation in storage and preparation.

**What documents should be requested:** the facility's HACCP plan (required for any establishment beyond the primary-production stage — verify this scope boundary before requesting it from a facility that may genuinely be primary production only); worker medical-examination records (cross-reference Chapter 5); traceability/receiving records (supplier, date, batch); the most recent internal or third-party microbiological test results where the facility's activity and the 2016 joint order's scope require them.

**What measurements should be taken:** cold-chain temperature — this is the one sub-domain in this entire manual (alongside Chapter 1's field pH/temperature readings) where a direct, immediate field measurement is both legally meaningful and technically simple. A refrigerated-storage reading outside the compliant range is immediate, objective evidence.

**What evidence should be collected:** logged temperature reading (numeric); photographs of storage/preparation area organization and worker PPE; copy of the HACCP plan; copy of the most recent worker medical exam record.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** cold-chain temperatures within range and logged, food-contact surfaces suitable and clean, worker hygiene measures in place and evidently used, pest exclusion in place with no active infestation evidence, HACCP plan present and specific to the facility's actual process (not a generic template with no facility-specific detail).
- **Minor non-compliance:** HACCP plan present but administratively outdated (not reflecting a recent process change); minor documentation gaps in traceability records.
- **Major non-compliance:** cold-chain temperature outside range without an immediate corrective log entry; worker hygiene facilities present but evidently unused (no soap, no accessible handwashing station); HACCP plan generic/not facility-specific.
- **Critical non-compliance:** no HACCP plan at all where legally required (post-primary-production establishments); active pest infestation in a food-contact area; cold-chain failure combined with no corrective action taken.
- **Not applicable:** genuinely primary-production-stage activities are outside the HACCP obligation by the decree's own scope — verify this classification rather than assume it, since many SafeInspect facility types (processing, storage, retail preparation) fall on the "establishment" side of the boundary even when they're agriculturally adjacent (e.g., an abattoir processes; it does not itself raise livestock, and is not primary production).

## 6. Reference Values

**Cold-chain temperature ranges [PRATIQUE] — DEFINITIVELY confirmed absent from Décret 17-140 itself, via full-text review this session.** Articles 45–49 (the decree's cold-chain provisions) use only qualitative language throughout — "a temperature not entailing a health risk" ("une température n'entraînant pas de risque pour la santé"), never a specific degree value. **Article 48 explicitly and directly states:** "Les températures et les procédés de conservation par congélation, surgélation ou réfrigération des denrées alimentaires altérables ainsi que leurs durées de conservation sont fixés par arrêté conjoint du ministre chargé de la protection du consommateur et de la répression des fraudes et des ministres concernés" — the specific temperatures, preservation methods, and storage durations are set by a **separate joint ministerial order**, not this decree. **This resolves the earlier uncertainty about which article to search: none of them contain the figures, by the decree's own explicit design.** The specific joint order has not been identified by number/date — it may be the 4 October 2016 order (microbiological criteria) or the 7 May 2025 order (hygiene conditions) already found in Section 2, or a third, more specific one — this is now a precisely-scoped search for one specific joint ministerial order's content, not an open-ended search across the whole decree.

**HACCP obligation scope boundary [LOI] — newly confirmed, not previously reflected in the SafeInspect checklist or its audit.** Décret 17-140 excludes primary production from the HACCP mandate. Recommend the platform's facility-type-to-HACCP-requirement mapping be reviewed against this boundary specifically (see Chapter 6/Chapter 8 forthcoming for UPD's classification question) rather than applying HACCP uniformly or narrowly by habit.

**Worker medical examination interval [PRATIQUE — not independently re-confirmed this session].** The existing SafeInspect checklist's six-month interval was not re-verified against Loi 18-11 or Décret 93-120's specific text during this chapter's research pass (a targeted search returned only non-Algerian jurisdictions' equivalent rules). **This value should not be presented as [LOI] until Chapter 5's dedicated research pass either confirms or corrects it.**

**Microbiological criteria for food products [LOI, not yet extracted].** The joint ministerial order of 4 October 2016 sets these; specific pathogen/count thresholds were not retrieved during this drafting pass. Flagged for follow-up if the platform intends to build lab-result-comparison criteria (paralleling Chapter 1's Décret 06-141 model).

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on the SafeInspect checklist audit (Session 5): the most common structural issue found was not a hygiene failure itself but **duplicated and drifting criteria** — the same underlying cold-chain and hygiene concepts independently re-written in bakery- and cold-room-specific files alongside the shared food-safety baseline, creating two non-identical versions of the same check with no mechanism keeping them synchronized. From this chapter's legal research: a newly identified likely gap is HACCP-plan verification being applied inconsistently relative to the primary-production exclusion boundary, which the previous audit did not have visibility into.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** a HACCP plan that reads as a generic template with no facility-specific critical control points identified should be treated as materially deficient, not merely a minor documentation gap — the entire value of the HACCP approach is in its facility-specificity.
- **Common misinterpretation:** "primary production" is a narrower category than it may appear — an abattoir or food-processing facility is not primary production merely because it's agriculturally adjacent; verify against the decree's actual scope rather than assuming.
- **Good practice:** where a field temperature reading is available, use it as the primary evidence for cold-chain criteria — it is more objective and immediate than a visual "looks cold enough" assessment.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** any facility claiming HACCP exemption on primary-production grounds should have that claim verified against its actual declared activity in the technical file, not accepted at face value.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Active pest infestation in a food-contact area, or a combined cold-chain failure with no corrective log, should independently drive a **critical** classification given the direct foodborne-illness pathway — consistent with the platform's existing critical-override logic.
- Missing HACCP plan where legally required is **critical**, not merely major, given Décret 17-140's decree-level (not just guideline-level) mandate.
- Documentation-only gaps (outdated but present HACCP plan, incomplete traceability records) are appropriately **minor**, provided the physical hygiene controls are otherwise sound.
