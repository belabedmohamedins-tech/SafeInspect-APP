# CHAPTER 8 — SITE HYGIENE, PEST CONTROL & GENERAL PREMISES

*Final chapter of the planned 8-chapter manual.*

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** pest presence (rodents, insects) is a direct disease-vector and food-contamination pathway; general premises conditions (lighting, cleanable surfaces) are the baseline environment every other chapter's controls depend on.
- **Environmental impact:** minimal direct impact; overlaps with Chapter 2 where pest-control chemical use itself requires proper handling/disposal.
- **Administrative importance:** unlike every other chapter in this manual, this domain has **no dedicated Algerian pest-control decree** — a targeted search this session found none, consistent with the original SafeInspect audit's research. The legal basis here is entirely the general environmental-nuisance framework (Loi 03-10) plus, for food-adjacent facilities, Décret 17-140 (Chapter 4).
- **Safety implications:** minimal direct overlap with Chapter 3, except where pesticide storage itself becomes a hazardous-substance concern (cross-reference Chapter 2).

## 3. Scientific Background

Pest management follows the internationally standard Integrated Pest Management (IPM) model: exclusion (physical barriers, sealed gaps) is the first and most effective line of defense, monitoring (traps, inspection) provides early detection before an infestation becomes established, and treatment (licensed pesticide application) is the last-resort corrective measure — treatment-only approaches without exclusion/monitoring tend to produce recurring rather than resolved infestations. General-premises conditions (lighting, cleanable floor/wall surfaces) support every other chapter's controls: inadequate lighting directly impairs an inspector's (and a worker's) ability to detect the physical evidence (spills, damage, pest activity) that most other chapters' visual criteria depend on.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** physical evidence of pest activity (droppings, gnaw marks, insect presence); structural exclusion measures (door seals, screens, sealed gaps); trap placement and condition where present; general lighting adequacy; floor/wall surface condition (cleanable, no unsealed porous material where the activity requires it).

**What documents should be requested:** the pest-control program documentation (written IPM program, whether self-managed or contracted) and, where a contractor is used, evidence of the contractor's service record — **note: unlike Chapter 2's hazardous-waste-collector accreditation, no specific Algerian pest-control-operator accreditation regime was confirmed to exist**, so this is a service-quality verification, not a license-verification exercise.

**What measurements should be taken:** none — this chapter, like Chapter 3, is entirely observational/documentary.

**What evidence should be collected:** photographs of any pest activity evidence, trap placement, and structural exclusion measures; copy of the written pest-control program and most recent service/intervention log.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** no active infestation evidence; written pest-control program in place with a current intervention log; structural exclusion measures intact; general premises (lighting, surfaces) adequate for the activity.
- **Minor non-compliance:** pest-control program present but intervention log administratively overdue; minor lighting/surface deficiencies not affecting food-contact or hazard-visibility areas.
- **Major non-compliance:** structural exclusion gaps (unsealed openings, damaged screens) with no active infestation yet observed; pest-control program present but generic/undocumented as actually executed.
- **Critical non-compliance:** active infestation evidence in a food-contact or hazardous-material storage area (cross-reference Chapter 4/Chapter 2 for the compounding classification).
- **Not applicable:** rare in this chapter — general premises and basic pest exclusion are universally relevant across all classified-establishment activity types in scope.

## 6. Reference Values

**No numeric Algerian legal values identified for this chapter** — this is the one chapter in the manual with no [LOI]-tagged quantitative table, consistent with Section 2's confirmed absence of a dedicated technical regulation. All practice-level guidance here should be tagged **[PRATIQUE]** or **[INTL]**:

- **Trap density / inspection frequency [INTL]:** standard IPM practice recommends a documented trap map with periodic (commonly monthly) inspection intervals — this is international guidance, not Algerian legal requirement, and should be presented to inspectors as such.
- **Structural gap-sealing standard [PRATIQUE]:** no specific maximum gap size was located in Algerian regulation; general practice (rodent exclusion typically requires sealing gaps above roughly 6mm) is cited internationally but not confirmed as Algerian law.
- **General lighting adequacy [PRATIQUE, per Décret 91-05's general workplace standard — cross-reference Chapter 5]:** Décret 91-05 requires a "clean, comfortable, healthy" workplace generally but this manual did not locate a specific lux-level lighting standard.

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on the SafeInspect checklist audit (Session 9): **this is the category with the most severe internal duplication found across the entire audit**, not a compliance-content problem — the same pest-control concept was independently re-derived across up to four overlapping layers (general baseline, food-specific baseline, and multiple facility-specific versions) within the app's checklist, producing up to 10–12 redundant questions on a single inspection for couvoir and UPD facility types. This chapter's legal research found no new compliance-content gap to add on top of that finding — the priority action for this domain remains the **consolidation** identified in the original audit, not new criteria.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** a pest-control program that is treatment-only (pesticide application records) with no exclusion or monitoring evidence — indicates a reactive rather than preventive approach, more likely to have recurring undetected infestations between inspections.
- **Common misinterpretation:** a signed pest-control service contract, without a corresponding intervention log, does not prove the program is actually being executed — the same evidentiary standard established in Chapter 2 for hazardous-waste collection contracts applies here.
- **Good practice:** treat general-premises lighting adequacy as an enabling condition for every other chapter's visual criteria, not an isolated checkbox — poor lighting genuinely degrades the reliability of every other visual finding made during the same inspection.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** active infestation evidence found in or near a food-contact or hazardous-material area should immediately trigger cross-reference to Chapter 4 or Chapter 2's compliance criteria, since the compounding risk (contamination pathway) is more severe than either chapter's finding taken alone.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Active infestation in a food-contact or hazardous-storage area should independently drive a **critical** classification via cross-reference to the relevant substantive chapter (4 or 2), not be scored as a standalone pest-control finding alone.
- Structural exclusion gaps with no active infestation are appropriately **major** — a real vulnerability, not yet a realized harm.
- Documentation-only gaps (log overdue, program present) are **minor**, provided physical exclusion is intact.

## 11. Related Requirements

- **Chapter 2 (Solid & Hazardous Waste):** pesticide storage/disposal and open waste as a pest-attractant are shared concerns — cross-reference rather than duplicate.
- **Chapter 4 (Food Safety):** food-adjacent facility types' pest requirements are governed there (Décret 17-140); this chapter provides the universal baseline every facility type gets regardless of food-adjacency.
- **All other chapters:** general-premises lighting/surface adequacy is an enabling condition for the visual evidence standards used throughout this manual.
