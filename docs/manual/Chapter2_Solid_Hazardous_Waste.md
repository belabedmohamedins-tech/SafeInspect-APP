# CHAPTER 2 — SOLID & HAZARDOUS WASTE
## النفايات الصلبة والخاصة الخطرة

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** improperly stored or mixed hazardous waste (chemical, biological, sharps) creates direct exposure risk to workers, waste handlers, and the surrounding public.
- **Environmental impact:** uncontrolled hazardous waste is a primary contamination pathway to soil and groundwater; improperly incinerated waste is an air-quality pathway (cross-reference Chapter 7).
- **Administrative importance:** Algeria operates a full, coded waste-classification and chain-of-custody regulatory system (Décret 06-104's waste list, the declaration/collection/transport/treatment permit chain) — one of the more procedurally complete categories in Algerian environmental law.
- **Safety implications:** mixed incompatible waste (e.g., oxidizers with combustibles) is a fire/explosion risk; cross-reference Chapter 3 (Fire Safety).

## 3. Scientific Background

Waste is classified in Algerian law (Décret 06-104) by a coded system analogous to internationally-used waste catalogues. Each waste type receives a code that determines its handling regime (ordinary/household, inert, special, special hazardous). This classification is the legal backbone of the entire chapter — every subsequent obligation (declaration, collection accreditation, transport conditions, treatment-facility acceptance) is triggered by which code a given waste stream falls under.

An inspection that never asks "what is the waste code for what this facility produces" is inspecting storage and containers without ever verifying the facility has correctly classified what it is storing.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** waste storage areas (segregation by category, container integrity, labeling), evidence of mixing between ordinary and hazardous streams, evidence of on-site burning (scorch marks, ash accumulation — cross-reference Chapter 3).

**What documents should be requested, in order:**
1. The facility's waste inventory/classification record — does it identify which Décret 06-104 code(s) apply to its actual waste streams?
2. The collection contract with an accredited operator (accreditation under Décret 09-19 — request the operator's accreditation certificate, not just the service contract).
3. The most recent transfer/pickup receipt or manifest — proves the contract is being executed, not merely signed.
4. Where the facility itself transports its own waste: the transport authorization documents specified in Section 6 below.
5. For healthcare-adjacent facilities: verify the three-stream color-coded segregation.

**What measurements should be taken:** none — this chapter is almost entirely documentary/visual; no Décret 06-104-linked quantitative field measurement was identified.

**What evidence should be collected:** photographs of segregated storage; copy of waste inventory/classification record; copy of collector's accreditation certificate; copy of most recent transfer manifest.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** waste classified by code, segregated accordingly, collected under contract with an accredited operator (accreditation verified, not assumed), transfer manifests current.
- **Minor non-compliance:** classification record incomplete or outdated but physical segregation and collection otherwise adequate.
- **Major non-compliance:** hazardous waste stored without segregation from ordinary waste; collection contract present but no transfer manifest available; collector's accreditation not verifiable.
- **Critical non-compliance:** hazardous waste stored with no evidence of any collection arrangement; evidence of on-site burning of waste; evidence of mixing of incompatible hazardous categories creating immediate fire/toxic-release risk; healthcare-adjacent facility with no color-coded stream segregation for infectious/sharps/body-part waste.
- **Not applicable:** facility generates no waste beyond ordinary municipal-equivalent waste (verify against the technical file).

**Décret 03-478 — Healthcare-activity waste three-stream color-coded system [LOI]:**
- Stream 1 (yellow): infectious/biological/anatomical waste and sharps
- Stream 2 (black): ordinary non-infectious healthcare waste (assimilable to household waste)
- Stream 3 (specific container): chemical/pharmaceutical waste

**Décret 04-410 — Waste treatment facility admission conditions [LOI]:**
- Prior acceptance certificate required, valid **1 year**, based on a technical file including: waste origin, producer/holder identity, pretreatment operations, chemical composition, collection/transport method, incompatible-substance risks, lab test results — **at the generator/holder's expense**
- Daily admission register: waste origin/nature, generator identity, transporter identity, vehicle registration number, weight, date/time, admission-control results, acceptance certificate reference
- Retention: **5-year** retention of acceptance certificates
- Closure dossier submitted **at least 6 months** before ceasing operations

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on the SafeInspect checklist audit (Session 3): only one facility type (UAB) in the current checklist actually performs waste classification verification. The most common structural gap is verifying only container/bin presence without ever checking which Décret 06-104 code applies. The SafeInspect checklist's "contract with an approved operator" criteria cite the wrong decree — the correct citation is Décret 09-19, not Décret 04-409.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** a pest-control program that is treatment-only with no exclusion or monitoring evidence.
- **Common misinterpretation:** a signed collection contract without a corresponding transfer manifest does not prove the contract is being executed — the same evidentiary standard applies here as in Chapter 4 for HACCP plan execution.
- **Good practice:** ask for the most recent transfer manifest before the contract — if it exists, it proves execution; if the operator can only produce the contract, probe further.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** a healthcare-adjacent facility (veterinary, abattoir with on-site veterinary practice) with no three-stream color-coded segregation is a critical finding even if the overall waste management otherwise appears adequate.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Evidence of on-site burning or mixing of incompatible hazardous categories should independently drive a **critical** classification regardless of aggregate score, given the immediate fire/toxic-release risk — mirrors the critical-override logic in `scoringUtils.ts`.
- A collection contract with no transfer manifest should be treated as **major**, not merely **minor** — the absence of proof-of-execution is functionally equivalent to not knowing whether the waste is being handled at all.
- Missing classification/inventory alone (with physical segregation and collection otherwise adequate) is appropriately **minor** — a documentary gap, not an active harm pathway.

## 11. Related Requirements

- **Chapter 1 (Wastewater):** septic-system sludge and oil-separator waste become special/hazardous waste once removed from the water system — governed by this chapter's rules, not Chapter 1's.
- **Chapter 3 (Fire Safety):** incompatible hazardous waste mixing and on-site incineration are simultaneously waste-management and fire-safety findings; cross-reference rather than double-count.
- **Chapter 7 (Air Quality):** on-site waste burning is also an uncontrolled-emissions event; cross-reference before treating it as a waste-chapter-only finding.
- **Documentation & Licensing (Chapter 6):** the waste-collection contract and transport authorization are licensing instruments for the collector/transporter, not the generating facility — verify which party's documentation is actually being checked.
