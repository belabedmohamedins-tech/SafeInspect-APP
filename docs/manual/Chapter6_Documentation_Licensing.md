# CHAPTER 6 — DOCUMENTATION & LICENSING
## الهوية والوثائق والتراخيص

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health / environmental / safety impact:** indirect but foundational — every other chapter's substantive findings are downstream of whether the facility is operating within a legally recognized and reviewed framework at all. An unlicensed facility has, by definition, never had its environmental impact, risk profile, or technical file reviewed by any authority.
- **Administrative importance:** this is the highest-leverage chapter in the manual precisely because it gates everything else — a licensing finding can trigger closure, which moots every other chapter's findings for that visit.
- **Safety implications:** the licensing procedure itself (Section 2) is where risk studies, impact studies, and category classification are actually determined — meaning several other chapters' "is a risk study required" questions are answered here, not independently.

## 3. Scientific Background

Not directly applicable in the technical/scientific sense — this chapter is procedural/administrative rather than grounded in a physical-science hazard model. Its "science" is regulatory design: a category system that scales licensing authority and procedural weight (ministerial → wali → commune → mere declaration) to the facility's actual environmental risk tier.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** physical evidence the facility's actual scale/activity matches what's declared in its license/declaration (production volume, equipment, storage quantities visibly consistent with the file).

**What documents should be requested, in order:**
1. The operating license or Category 4 declaration receipt, matched to the facility's actual Décret 07-144 category.
2. Where the facility's category requires one: the EIA study/impact summary (Décret 07-145) and/or risk study (Décret 06-198 art. 12–14).
3. Any sector-specific license not substituted by the above (per art. 4's explicit non-substitution rule) — e.g., Chapter 3's fire-safety wilaya authorization.
4. **Where the facility has no license/declaration at all: check whether it may fall within the Décret 24-196 three-year regularization grace period (running from 11 June 2024, expiring approximately June 2027) before defaulting to a critical unlicensed-operation finding.**

**What measurements should be taken:** none — entirely documentary.

**What evidence should be collected:** copies of the license/declaration, EIA/risk study where applicable, and any sector-specific license.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** license/declaration held, matches the correct Décret 07-144 category, EIA/risk study on file where required, sector-specific licenses (fire safety, etc.) also held.
- **Minor non-compliance:** license held but administrative detail (address, declared capacity) outdated relative to current operations.
- **Major non-compliance:** license category mismatched to actual activity/scale; EIA/risk study missing where the facility's category requires one.
- **Critical non-compliance:** no license or declaration at all, AND the facility does not fall within the Décret 24-196 regularization grace period; operating despite a suspension/closure order.
- **Not applicable:** N/A does not meaningfully apply — every facility in scope is a classified establishment subject to some tier of this framework.

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Unlicensed operation is very likely the single most common finding in this entire manual — the Blida statistic (~25% of counted classified establishments unlicensed) supports this directly. The original audit also found that every one of the eighteen SafeInspect facility-type checklists independently duplicated a license-validity check rather than using one shared, category-aware criterion.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** a facility whose declared category doesn't match its observed scale — e.g., a Category 4 declaration-only facility with production volume or equipment suggesting Category 2 or 3 should have applied.
- **Common misinterpretation:** holding one type of license (e.g., the Décret 06-198 environmental operating license) does not mean the facility is licensed for everything — art. 4 explicitly preserves the need for separate sectoral licenses.
- **Good practice:** for any unlicensed facility, check the Décret 24-196 grace-period window before defaulting to the most severe classification.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** a facility operating despite a formally issued suspension or closure order is a fundamentally different (and more severe) finding than simply lacking a license.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- No license/declaration, **outside** any applicable grace period → **critical** classification, shortest re-inspection interval.
- No license/declaration, **within** the confirmed Décret 24-196 grace period → **major** finding requiring regularization-status follow-up.
- Category mismatch (operating beyond declared category) → **major**.

## 11. Related Requirements

- **Chapter 1 (Wastewater):** the discharge permit is a sector-specific license under art. 4's non-substitution rule — verify separately.
- **Chapter 3 (Fire Safety):** the Loi 19-02 wilaya fire-safety operating-user authorization is likewise a separate, non-substitutable license.
- **All other chapters:** whether a risk study or EIA is required is determined by this chapter's licensing procedure (art. 5) — cross-reference rather than independently re-deriving per chapter.
