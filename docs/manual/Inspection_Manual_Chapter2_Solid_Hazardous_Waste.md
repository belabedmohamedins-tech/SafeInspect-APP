# CHAPTER 2 — SOLID & HAZARDOUS WASTE
## النفايات الصلبة والخاصة الخطرة

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** improperly stored or mixed hazardous waste (chemical, biological, sharps) creates direct exposure risk to workers, waste handlers, and — where uncollected — the surrounding public.
- **Environmental impact:** uncontrolled hazardous waste is a primary contamination pathway to soil and groundwater, and improperly incinerated waste is an air-quality pathway (cross-reference Chapter 7).
- **Administrative importance:** Algeria operates a full, coded waste-classification and chain-of-custody regulatory system (Décret 06-104's waste list, the declaration/collection/transport/treatment permit chain) — this is one of the more procedurally complete categories in Algerian environmental law, and inspection should verify the actual chain, not just the presence of bins.
- **Safety implications:** mixed incompatible waste (e.g., oxidizers with combustibles) is a fire/explosion risk; cross-reference Chapter 3 (Fire Safety).

## 3. Scientific Background

Waste is classified in Algerian law (Décret 06-104) by a coded system analogous in structure to internationally-used waste catalogues: each waste type receives a code that determines its handling regime (ordinary/household, inert, special, special hazardous). This classification is the scientific and legal backbone of the entire chapter — every subsequent obligation (declaration, collection accreditation, transport conditions, treatment-facility acceptance) is triggered by which code a given waste stream falls under. An inspection that never asks "what is the waste code for what this facility produces" is inspecting storage and containers without ever verifying the facility has correctly classified what it's storing.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** waste storage areas (segregation by category, container integrity, labeling), evidence of mixing between ordinary and hazardous streams, evidence of on-site burning (scorch marks, ash accumulation — cross-reference Chapter 3).

**What documents should be requested, in order:**
1. The facility's waste inventory/classification record — does it identify which Décret 06-104 code(s) apply to its actual waste streams?
2. The collection contract with an accredited operator (accreditation under Décret 09-19 — request the operator's accreditation certificate, not just the service contract).
3. The most recent transfer/pickup receipt or manifest — proves the contract is being executed, not merely signed.
4. Where the facility itself transports its own waste rather than using a third-party collector: the transport authorization documents specified in Section 6 below.
5. For healthcare-adjacent facilities (veterinary, abattoir with on-site veterinary practice): verify the three-stream color-coded segregation described in Section 6.

**What measurements should be taken:** none — this chapter is almost entirely documentary/visual.

**What evidence should be collected:** photographs of segregated storage; copy of waste inventory/classification record; copy of collector's accreditation certificate; copy of most recent transfer manifest.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** waste classified by code, segregated accordingly, collected under contract with an accredited operator (accreditation verified, not assumed), transfer manifests current.
- **Minor non-compliance:** classification record incomplete or outdated but physical segregation and collection otherwise adequate.
- **Major non-compliance:** hazardous waste stored without segregation from ordinary waste; collection contract present but no transfer manifest available; collector's accreditation not verifiable.
- **Critical non-compliance:** hazardous waste stored with no evidence of any collection arrangement at all; evidence of on-site burning of waste; evidence of mixing of incompatible hazardous categories; healthcare-adjacent facility with no color-coded stream segregation for infectious/sharps/body-part waste.
- **Not applicable:** facility generates no waste beyond ordinary municipal-equivalent waste (verify against the technical file).

| 9 | Loading and unloading point identification |

**Healthcare-activity waste — 3-stream color-coded system [LOI], Décret 03-478:**

| Stream | Color | Waste type |
|---|---|---|
| Stream 1 | **Green** | Body parts, anatomical waste |
| Stream 2 | **Yellow** | Infectious waste, sharps |
| Stream 3 | **Red** | Toxic/chemical waste |

**Applicability note:** This system is confirmed as applicable to healthcare activities. For abattoirs and facilities with on-site veterinary practice, the three-stream system is the applicable standard — **this is a genuine new finding with zero current coverage in the SafeInspect checklist.** Recommend adding a dedicated criterion for healthcare-adjacent facility types.

**Waste treatment facility admission conditions [LOI], Décret 04-410:**
- A **prior acceptance certificate**, valid **1 year**, based on a technical file
- A **daily admission register** logging waste origin/nature, generator identity, transporter identity, vehicle registration, weight, date/time, and acceptance certificate reference
- **5-year retention** of acceptance certificates

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on audit Session 3:
- Waste classification step entirely skipped — inspections verify containers and storage without ever checking the waste code, meaning the correct handling regime is never confirmed.
- "Contract with approved collector" criteria not citing Décret 09-19, meaning the criterion's verification standard (accreditation, not just a service contract) is underspecified.
- Healthcare-adjacent waste (veterinary, abattoir) has zero coverage in the current checklist despite Décret 03-478's confirmed applicability.
- Décret 04-409 mis-cited for non-transport obligations (LPG/C, SDS) — confirmed error in the codebase, flagged for Phase 2 correction.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** a collection service contract with no corresponding transfer manifests — the contract may have been signed for compliance appearance without actual waste being collected.
- **Common misinterpretation:** "ordinary municipal waste" classification applied to waste streams that, based on the facility's actual activity, are almost certainly special/hazardous under Décret 06-104 — verify the classification, don't accept it at face value.
- **Good practice:** where a healthcare-adjacent activity is present (veterinary practice, medical waste generation), immediately check for the Décret 03-478 three-stream system before moving on to other waste criteria.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Evidence of on-site burning or mixing of incompatible hazardous categories should independently drive a **critical** classification regardless of the facility's aggregate score.
- A collection contract with no transfer manifest should be treated as **major**, not merely **minor**.
- Missing classification/inventory alone (with physical segregation and collection otherwise adequate) is appropriately **minor**.

## 11. Related Requirements

- **Chapter 1 (Wastewater):** septic-system sludge and oil-separator waste become special/hazardous waste once removed from the water system — governed by this chapter, not Chapter 1.
- **Chapter 3 (Fire Safety):** incompatible hazardous waste mixing and on-site incineration are simultaneously waste-management and fire-safety findings.
- **Chapter 7 (Air Quality):** on-site waste burning is also an uncontrolled-emissions event.
