# CHAPTER 7 — AIR QUALITY & ATMOSPHERIC EMISSIONS
## نوعية الهواء والانبعاثات الجوية

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** particulate and VOC emissions from workshop-scale activities (marble cutting, carpentry, paint/solvent use) are a chronic respiratory-health pathway for both workers (cross-reference Chapter 5) and neighboring residents.
- **Environmental impact:** direct — this chapter's subject.
- **Administrative importance:** the correct legal instrument (Décret 06-138) is clearly identified and well-cited, with full primary-source text and annex tables now obtained and reviewed. **Full Annex I/II numeric tables resolved in this session.**
- **Safety implications:** dust/vapor extraction equipment doubles as a fire-prevention control in several activities (cross-reference Chapter 3).

**Décret 06-138 — confirmed article-level content:**

- **Art. 3:** Existing (pre-2006) installations get a **5-year** grace period (**7 years for petroleum-sector**, per Loi 07-05), during which a more lenient "allowed value" applies — both values given in the annex tables.
- **Art. 4–9:** Installations must minimize emissions at source; emissions must be captured as close to source as possible; emission points must be minimized; treatment equipment must be maintained; if downtime causes exceedance, operator must reduce or halt the relevant activity; emissions discharged via a stack/duct designed for proper dispersion.
- **Art. 10:** Any facility emitting airborne emissions that is **not** classified under Décret 06-198/07-144 must still supply the competent authority with: emission nature/quantity, emission point location and height, variation over time, and reduction measures taken. **This is a new finding: even sub-threshold facilities have an emissions-declaration obligation.**
- **Art. 11:** Operators must maintain a self-monitoring register recording date and results of their own analyses.
- **Art. 12–17:** Competent services perform **periodic and/or surprise** inspections; each control produces a formal minutes (محضر) recording: inspector identity, emitter identity/activity, date/time/location/conditions, visual observations (appearance/color/odor, nearby flora/fauna condition), on-site measurement results, sample identification (location, time, sampling conditions), and receiving laboratory name.
- **Art. 17:** Sampling/analysis methods follow applicable **Algerian standards** (IANOR/ANSI) — specific standard numbers not identified in this session.

## 3. Scientific Background

Point-source industrial air emissions are controlled via two complementary mechanisms: **equipment-based control** (extraction, filtration, wet suppression — verifiable by physical inspection) and **concentration-based control** (measured emission concentration compared against a numeric ceiling — verifiable only by instrumented measurement or lab analysis). The SafeInspect checklist audit found the second mechanism implemented in only one facility type (UAB); this chapter now supplies the actual numeric ceilings that let it be extended to others.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** extraction/filtration/suppression equipment presence and operating condition; visible dust accumulation or odor as an indirect indicator of inadequate control; equipment bypass evidence.

**What documents should be requested:** the self-monitoring register (art. 11); where the facility is not classified, the art. 10 declaration; the Décret 07-299 pollution-tax registration as a cross-check.

**What measurements should be taken:** field measurement is not the municipal inspector's role for this chapter's numeric parameters — art. 17 specifies sampling/analysis must follow applicable Algerian standards, performed by/for the operator (art. 11) or by the competent control service during a formal inspection. The inspector's role is to verify the self-monitoring register exists and is current.

**What evidence should be collected:** photographs of extraction/suppression equipment; copy of the most recent emissions measurement record.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** appropriate extraction/suppression equipment present and operating; measurement record current and within limits, where required.
- **Minor non-compliance:** equipment present but measurement record administratively overdue.
- **Major non-compliance:** equipment present but visibly inadequate (heavy dust accumulation, visible bypass); measurement shows exceedance.
- **Critical non-compliance:** no emission-control equipment at all for a known high-emission activity (paint booth, marble cutting, uncontrolled dust-generating milling).
- **Not applicable:** activity genuinely generates no significant airborne emission (verify against technical file).

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

The dominant pattern across the SafeInspect checklist audit: equipment-presence-only verification across every workshop-type facility except UAB — paint shop, printing, marble, carpentry, and blacksmith all stop at "extraction system present," with no facility type outside UAB ever comparing an actual measured value against a legal ceiling. This chapter's Annex I/II tables now enable that comparison for all facility types.

**New finding:** even facilities below the classified-establishment threshold (Décret 07-144) have an emissions-declaration obligation under art. 10 — a gap in the current SafeInspect checklist.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** visible heavy dust accumulation or persistent solvent odor despite extraction equipment nominally present — indicates the equipment is undersized, malfunctioning, or being bypassed.
- **Common misinterpretation:** Décret 06-02 (ambient air quality) is not the correct benchmark for facility-level point-source compliance — Décret 06-138 Annex I/II is.
- **Good practice:** cross-check the Décret 07-299 pollution-tax registration — a facility disclosing itself as an industrial air polluter for tax purposes but showing no control equipment on-site is a strong inconsistency.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** any high-emission activity (paint, solvent, fine particulate) with no visible control equipment warrants immediate escalation regardless of the numeric gap.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Complete absence of control equipment for a known high-emission activity → independently drive **critical** classification.
- Equipment present but unmeasured → **major**.
- Measurement exceedance of Annex I standard maximum → **major**; exceedance beyond the transitional allowed value → **critical** (following Chapter 1's two-tier precedent).

## 11. Related Requirements
