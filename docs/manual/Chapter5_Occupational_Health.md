# CHAPTER 5 — OCCUPATIONAL HEALTH & WORKER PROTECTION
## الصحة والسلامة المهنية

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** this chapter protects the workforce specifically, as distinct from Chapters 1–4's public/environmental protection — chronic occupational exposure (dust, noise, chemical) produces cumulative harm that a single inspection visit cannot detect without the right documentary trail (medical surveillance records).
- **Environmental impact:** minimal direct overlap, except where worker-protection equipment (extraction, ventilation) doubles as an environmental-emissions control (cross-reference Chapter 6).
- **Administrative importance:** Algeria regulates this domain through a layered structure — a general framework law (88-07), a general implementing decree (91-05), a medical-surveillance-specific decree (93-120), a training-specific decree (02-427), and increasingly, **substance-specific targeted decrees** (a lead-exposure decree was confirmed as recently issued during this manual's drafting period) — meaning the general framework sets obligations, but binding numeric exposure limits are more likely to be found in a specific substance/hazard decree than in the general texts.
- **Safety implications:** shares its legal foundation (Loi 88-07, Décret 91-05) directly with Chapter 3's fire-ignition-source prevention.

## 3. Scientific Background

Occupational exposure follows a fundamentally different harm model than most other chapters in this manual: the harm is cumulative and dose-dependent rather than acute, meaning a single inspection visit cannot directly observe the harm — it can only verify the presence of controls (PPE, extraction, medical surveillance) that are presumed, based on established occupational-medicine science, to keep cumulative exposure below a harmful threshold. This is why medical surveillance records (Décret 93-120) carry particular evidentiary weight in this chapter: a periodic blood-lead test or audiometric exam is the closest this domain gets to a direct, individual-level harm measurement, analogous to Chapter 1's lab analysis or Chapter 4's temperature log.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** PPE presence and correct type for the activity's actual hazard profile; machine guards on moving equipment; general workplace conditions specified in Décret 91-05 (changing rooms, washbasins, drinking water, canteen hygiene).

**What documents should be requested:** worker medical-examination records (verify against whatever interval is ultimately confirmed — see Section 6's open flag); evidence of PPE-use training under Décret 02-427 (a sign-off sheet, training record, or equivalent — this is a newly-recommended evidence type); for any facility handling a substance covered by a targeted exposure-limit decree (lead confirmed; others may exist), the substance-specific declaration and monitoring records.

**What measurements should be taken:** where field equipment permits, a noise-level reading is the most direct verification available for the noise-protection obligation confirmed in Section 2 — even without a confirmed Algerian numeric ceiling, a measured value creates an auditable record and a basis for comparison once the ceiling is located.

**What evidence should be collected:** photographs of PPE in use (not just present in storage); copy of medical-exam records; copy of training/instruction records; for substance-specific obligations, copy of the required declaration and monitoring records.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** PPE present, correct type, and evidence of actual use (not just storage); machine guards in place; medical-exam records current; workplace conditions (changing rooms, sanitation, drinking water) meet Décret 91-05's general standard.
- **Minor non-compliance:** medical-exam records administratively overdue with no other active risk indicator.
- **Major non-compliance:** PPE absent or wrong type for the actual hazard; machine guard absent; no evidence of PPE-use training.
- **Critical non-compliance:** active exposure to an unprotected serious hazard (unguarded moving machinery in active use, no respiratory protection in a confirmed high-dust/vapor environment); no medical surveillance at all for a substance covered by a targeted exposure-limit decree (e.g., lead) where the activity plausibly involves that substance.
- **Not applicable:** activity genuinely does not present the specific hazard in question (verify rather than assume).

## 6. Reference Values

**Occupational noise-exposure limit — [INTL, adopted in practice].** Décret exécutif n° 93-184 (27 juillet 1993) sets maximum **ambient/neighbor-nuisance** noise levels (residential/industrial zone limits, 35–45 dB(A) at night in residential zones) — it is NOT an occupational in-plant worker-exposure standard. No dedicated Algerian occupational noise-exposure decree was confirmed. However, an Algerian occupational-risk-prevention body (OPREBATP) explicitly cites the WHO reference standard — **85 dB(A) over 8 hours/day, 5 days/week** — as the occupational-deafness threshold in its published guidance, suggesting this WHO figure is the de facto reference in Algerian occupational-health practice. **Tag as [INTL, adopted in practice] — not [LOI] and not [SILENCE].**

**Worker medical-examination interval — [research gap].** Loi 18-11 confirms the obligation exists and delegates the interval to implementing regulation. The SafeInspect checklist's existing **six-month figure** was not confirmed or denied — it may originate from Décret 93-120. Retain at [PRATIQUE] status until the specific implementing regulation is located.

**PPE-training requirement — [LOI].** Décret 02-427 confirms a real legal obligation for worker training/instruction in occupational-risk prevention. This directly closes the audit's PPE-training gap finding with an actual legal citation — upgrade from best-practice recommendation to a [LOI]-grounded criterion.

**Blood-lead ceiling values — [LOI, exists, not retrieved].** Confirmed to exist within the recently-issued lead-exposure decree; specific numeric ceiling not retrieved. Relevant primarily to facility types plausibly handling lead (soldering, certain metalworking).

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on the SafeInspect checklist audit: the most common structural gap was PPE required without any corresponding measurement or training verification — hearing protection specified in several criteria with no noise measurement to justify or calibrate it, and no criterion anywhere verifying workers were actually trained to use the PPE they're required to wear. This chapter strengthens both findings: the noise gap is now understood to likely have a real, specific Algerian numeric answer not yet located, and the training gap now has a specific legal citation (Décret 02-427).

**Red flag for auditors:** PPE present in original packaging or clearly unused (dust-free, tags still attached) despite the facility being operational — indicates the equipment was acquired for inspection-compliance purposes rather than actual use.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** PPE present in original packaging or clearly unused despite the facility being operational.
- **Common misinterpretation:** the general noise-protection obligation in Décret 91-05/Loi 18-11 is sometimes treated as merely aspirational because no ceiling number is commonly cited — a specific numeric standard very likely exists in a targeted instrument not yet widely referenced.
- **Good practice:** where a substance-specific targeted decree exists (lead confirmed; verify for others), treat it as the authoritative source over the general framework texts.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** any facility whose activity plausibly involves lead, other heavy metals, or high-noise processes should be checked against the possibility that a substance-specific decree with binding numeric limits applies.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Active unprotected exposure to a serious, immediately hazardous condition should independently drive a **critical** classification.
- Missing PPE-use training (as distinct from missing PPE itself) is appropriately **major**, not critical.
- Medical-surveillance gaps should be treated as **major** where the activity involves a confirmed targeted-decree substance (lead), and **minor** where the exam obligation is the general periodic requirement with no elevated substance-specific risk.

## 11. Related Requirements

- **Chapter 3 (Fire Safety):** shares its legal foundation (Loi 88-07, Décret 91-05) directly — PPE and machine-guard criteria should be cross-referenced.
- **Chapter 4 (Food Safety):** worker medical-exam and hygiene criteria overlap directly with Loi 18-11's food-service-specific medical-exam obligation — the two chapters should share a single verified interval once Section 6's open item is resolved.
- **Chapter 6 (Air Quality):** dust/fume extraction equipment serves simultaneously as a worker-protection control (this chapter) and an environmental-emissions control (Chapter 6) — assess once, cross-reference, not independently re-derived in both chapters.
