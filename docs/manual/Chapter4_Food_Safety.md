# CHAPTER 4 — FOOD SAFETY & HYGIENE
## سلامة وصحة الأغذية

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** this chapter's inspection domain (food-contact surfaces, cold-chain control, worker hygiene, pest exclusion) directly governs the primary transmission pathways for foodborne pathogens — the highest-consequence, most time-sensitive risk category in this manual after Chapter 3's life-safety findings.
- **Environmental impact:** secondary — food-processing waste streams are governed primarily by Chapter 2.
- **Administrative importance:** Décret 17-140 is unusual in this manual in that it directly and explicitly mandates HACCP-based procedures **by law** (art. 9), not merely by technical guideline — giving HACCP-related inspection criteria a firmer legal basis than most other "international best practice" items referenced elsewhere.
- **Safety implications:** minimal direct overlap with Chapter 3, except where food-processing equipment introduces ignition sources (ovens, fryers).

## 3. Scientific Background

Food-hygiene inspection targets the same small set of transmission pathways in every jurisdiction: temperature abuse (allowing pathogen growth in the "danger zone" between refrigeration and cooking temperatures), cross-contamination (raw-to-ready-to-eat, or via unwashed hands/surfaces), and vector introduction (pests carrying pathogens into food-contact areas). Décret 17-140's HACCP mandate operationalizes control of all three by requiring the establishment to identify its own critical control points — meaning a well-implemented facility-specific HACCP plan is a stronger control than a fixed external checklist alone.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** cold-storage equipment and any visible temperature display; food-contact surface condition (smooth, non-porous, visibly clean); worker PPE and handwashing-station presence/use; visible pest activity or exclusion measures (screens, traps); raw/ready-to-eat product separation in storage and preparation.

**What documents should be requested:** the facility's HACCP plan (required for any establishment beyond the primary-production stage); worker medical-examination records (cross-reference Chapter 5); traceability/receiving records (supplier, date, batch); the most recent internal or third-party microbiological test results where applicable.

**What measurements should be taken:** cold-chain temperature — the one sub-domain in this entire manual (alongside Chapter 1's field pH/temperature readings) where a direct, immediate field measurement is both legally meaningful and technically simple.

**What evidence should be collected:** logged temperature reading (numeric); photographs of storage/preparation area organization and worker PPE; copy of the HACCP plan; copy of the most recent worker medical exam record.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** cold-chain temperatures within range and logged, food-contact surfaces suitable and clean, worker hygiene measures in place and evidently used, pest exclusion in place with no active infestation evidence, HACCP plan present and specific to the facility's actual process (not a generic template).
- **Minor non-compliance:** HACCP plan present but administratively outdated (not reflecting a recent process change); minor documentation gaps in traceability records.
- **Major non-compliance:** cold-chain temperature outside range without an immediate corrective log entry; worker hygiene facilities present but evidently unused; HACCP plan generic/not facility-specific.
- **Critical non-compliance:** no HACCP plan at all where legally required (post-primary-production establishments); active pest infestation in a food-contact area; cold-chain failure combined with no corrective action taken.
- **Not applicable:** genuinely primary-production-stage activities are outside the HACCP obligation by the decree's own scope — verify this classification rather than assume it.

## 6. Reference Values

**Cold-chain temperature ranges [PRATIQUE] — definitively confirmed absent from Décret 17-140 itself.** Arts. 45–49 use only qualitative language. Art. 48 explicitly states specific temperatures are set by a separate joint ministerial order (not yet identified by number/date). **Do not present any temperature value as [LOI] until that joint ministerial order is located and reviewed.**

**HACCP obligation scope boundary [LOI]:** Décret 17-140 art. 9 excludes primary production from the HACCP mandate. The platform's facility-type-to-HACCP-requirement mapping should be reviewed against this boundary:
- Abattoir: NOT primary production (it processes; it does not raise livestock) — HACCP applies
- UPD (livestock/poultry housing): potentially primary production — verify
- Couvoir (hatchery): potentially primary production — verify
- Bakery, food processing, cold room: NOT primary production — HACCP applies

**Worker medical examination interval [PRATIQUE — not confirmed as LOI]:** the existing SafeInspect checklist's six-month interval was not confirmed or denied during this chapter's research. Retain as [PRATIQUE] pending Chapter 5's dedicated research or article-level review of Décret 93-120.

**Microbiological criteria for food products [LOI — not yet extracted]:** Joint ministerial order of 4 October 2016 sets these; specific pathogen/count thresholds not retrieved during this drafting pass.

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on the SafeInspect checklist audit (Session 5): the most common structural issue is duplicated and drifting criteria — the same cold-chain and hygiene concepts independently re-written in bakery- and cold-room-specific files alongside the shared food-safety baseline, creating non-identical versions of the same check. A newly identified likely gap: HACCP-plan verification being applied inconsistently relative to the primary-production exclusion boundary, which the previous audit did not have visibility into.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** a HACCP plan that reads as a generic template with no facility-specific critical control points should be treated as materially deficient, not merely a minor documentation gap — the entire value of the HACCP approach is in its facility-specificity.
- **Common misinterpretation:** "primary production" is narrower than it appears — an abattoir is not primary production merely because it is agriculturally adjacent; verify against the decree's actual scope.
- **Good practice:** where a field temperature reading is available, use it as the primary evidence for cold-chain criteria — more objective and immediate than a visual "looks cold enough" assessment.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** any facility claiming HACCP exemption on primary-production grounds should have that claim verified against its actual declared activity in the technical file, not accepted at face value.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Active pest infestation in a food-contact area, or a combined cold-chain failure with no corrective log, should independently drive a **critical** classification.
- Missing HACCP plan where legally required is **critical**, not merely major, given Décret 17-140's decree-level (not guideline-level) mandate.
- Documentation-only gaps (outdated but present HACCP plan, incomplete traceability records) are appropriately **minor**, provided physical hygiene controls are otherwise sound.
