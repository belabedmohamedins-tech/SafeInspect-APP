# CHAPTER 3 — FIRE SAFETY & HAZARDOUS SUBSTANCES
## السلامة من الحريق والمواد الخطرة

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health / life-safety impact:** this is the one inspection category where a single missed criterion (a blocked exit, a non-functional extinguisher, an unmanaged flammable-gas store) can produce immediate mass-casualty consequences rather than cumulative long-term harm — Algeria's 2019 legislative overhaul of this domain (Loi 19-02, replacing a 1976 decree) was itself a direct legislative response to a series of fires, per the law's own stated rationale.
- **Environmental impact:** secondary — fire and explosion events themselves generate emissions and, where flammable/toxic chemical stock is involved, contamination events; cross-reference Chapter 7 (Air Quality) and Chapter 2 (Solid & Hazardous Waste).
- **Administrative importance:** Loi 19-02 created a formal two-tier enforcement structure (a national central committee and wilaya-level committees) and gave certain civil-protection officers judicial-police powers specifically for fire-safety violations — meaning a fire-safety finding can trigger a distinct enforcement pathway beyond the municipal inspection process itself.
- **Safety implications:** this chapter is definitionally about safety; see Sections 5–6 below for the specific risk pathways.

**Loi 19-02 — confirmed structural detail (full primary-source text obtained and reviewed this session, superseding the earlier secondary-source-based summary):**

*Covered categories [LOI], now precisely confirmed from the law's own definitions (art. 3):*
- **ERP (établissement recevant du public):** any establishment admitting persons freely, for payment, or for participation, or hosting meetings open to all persons by free or paid invitation.
- **High-rise building (عمارة مرتفعة):** a building whose lowest usable floor at the highest level sits **above 50m for residential buildings, or above 28m for all other buildings**, measured from the ground level usable by civil-protection apparatus.
- **Very-high-rise building (عمارة مرتفعة جدا):** a building whose lowest usable floor at the highest level sits **above 200m**. **This confirms the "200m" figure flagged as a likely transcription error in the previous version of this chapter was in fact correct — it is a real, distinct third category, not the general high-rise threshold. The earlier flag is retracted.**
- **Residential building:** building or portion with one or more dwellings where the lowest floor of the highest dwelling sits at or below 50m.
- **Sleeping establishment (محل للنوم):** any space arranged and designated for overnight sleeping.
- **Explicitly excluded (art. 45):** facilities and buildings belonging to the Ministry of National Defense.

*Residential building 4-category height classification (art. 17) [LOI], confirmed — relevant primarily for context, not directly to SafeInspect's commercial/industrial facility types:* Category 1 (single-story or single-story+1 individual houses, isolated or row-attached); Category 2 (multi-floor individual/collective houses up to 3 floors, highest-dwelling floor ≤8m); Category 3 (≤28m); Category 4 (28–50m). Specific security measures per category are set by implementing regulation, not in the law itself.

*Responsible parties (art. 8–10) [LOI]:* the law assigns distinct obligations to four actor categories — **builder** (مشيد), **equipment installer** (مركب تجهيزات), **owner** (مالك), and **operator** (مستغل) — each required to ensure materials, installations, and equipment are built/maintained per the law; builders/installers must document fire-reaction and resistance properties of materials used.

*Security note and risk-adjacent planning (art. 20–22) [LOI], confirmed:* any building/development/operating permit application for a covered facility must include a **"security note" (مذكرة أمنية)** — a technical document describing all protection/security measures — prepared by an **accredited specialized fire-safety design office** (accreditation procedure set by regulation, not detailed in the law). **Only Category-1 ERPs and high-rise/very-high-rise buildings** (not all ERPs) must additionally prepare a **prevention plan** (مخطط وقاية) and an **intervention plan** (مخطط تدخل), and must run periodic simulation drills with civil-protection services to test the intervention plan's effectiveness. Both plans require wali approval after civil-protection opinion.

*Operating license (art. 23–24) [LOI], confirmed:* operating an ERP or high-rise/very-high-rise building requires a license from the territorially competent **wali**, issued after the wilaya fire-prevention committee's opinion. Any change in the facility's declared activity requires a separate **regularization license** (رخصة تسوية) from the same wilaya committee.

*Committee structure (art. 25–29) [LOI], confirmed:* **Central Committee** (Ministry of Interior level) — national policy, advisory opinions, appeals from wilaya committee decisions. **Wilaya Committee** — studies license applications, **conducts inspection visits specifically when a facility opens or after construction works, prior to granting the operating license**, and proposes additional security measures.

*Enforcement personnel (art. 30–31) [LOI], confirmed:* in addition to judicial police officers, **designated civil-protection officers** are authorized to monitor and record violations under this law, after taking a formal oath.

*Sanctions ladder — now confirmed with exact figures and timeframes, not just a general structure [LOI]:*
- **Administrative (art. 33–34):** formal notice (إعذار) issued by the wali → if uncorrected within **6 months**, wali issues administrative closure for **up to 30 days maximum** → if the violation persists after that, **final withdrawal of the operating license**.
- **Penal (art. 35–43), by specific violation:**
  - Obstructing a civil-protection/police officer performing their duties: penalties under art. 148 of the Penal Code (art. 35).
  - Operating an ERP/high-rise without the required license, or changing declared activity without a regularization license: **6 months–2 years imprisonment and/or 500,000–1,000,000 DA fine** (art. 36).
  - Exceeding the facility's licensed maximum public capacity: **2 months–1 year imprisonment and/or 200,000–500,000 DA fine** (art. 37).
  - False declaration to obtain an operating license: penalties per the Penal Code (art. 38).
  - Blocking/disabling an emergency exit while the public is present: **6 months–2 years imprisonment and/or 500,000–1,000,000 DA fine** (art. 39).
  - Arranging unauthorized sleeping quarters within a covered facility: **200,000–500,000 DA fine** (art. 40).
  - Builder/installer whose materials don't match their certified fire-reaction properties: **2 months–1 year imprisonment and/or 200,000–500,000 DA fine** (art. 41).
  - Builder/installer failing to provide fire-reaction/resistance documentation at all: **400,000–1,000,000 DA fine** (art. 42).
  - Legal persons (companies) are criminally liable per the Penal Code for all of the above (art. 43).

*Existing-facility compliance deadline (art. 44) [LOI], confirmed — time-sensitive, unlike Chapter 6's still-active Décret 24-196 grace period:* all pre-existing establishments, buildings, and residences covered by this law had **5 years from the law's publication date (21 July 2019) to come into conformity — i.e., a deadline of approximately 21 July 2024.** **Given the current date, this deadline has already passed.** Unlike Chapter 6's active regularization window, this means a covered pre-existing facility found non-compliant with Loi 19-02 today has **no remaining grace period to invoke** — the transition period already closed roughly a year before this manual's drafting.

*Transitional continuity of old implementing texts (art. 46) [LOI], confirmed and directly relevant to Section 6's remaining gap:* implementing texts issued under the repealed 1976 ordinance **remain in force until new implementing texts under this 2019 law are published**. This means the still-missing specific numeric standards (ERP models/classes, building-material fire-reaction classifications, and by extension likely evacuation-capacity and extinguisher-count figures) may currently still be governed by **1976-era implementing texts**, not yet superseded — a concrete, different search target for closing Section 6's remaining gap, rather than assuming a 2019-era implementing text must exist.

## 3. Scientific Background

Fire requires three simultaneous elements (fuel, oxidizer, ignition source); prevention strategy accordingly separates into three corresponding control categories, all represented in this chapter's inspection criteria: **fuel-load control** (flammable material storage limits, segregation of incompatible substances — cross-reference Chapter 2 on hazardous-waste mixing), **ignition-source control** (electrical safety, no-open-flame rules near flammable stores, spark-free tooling in gas-handling areas), and **suppression/response capability** (extinguisher presence and type-matching, evacuation routes, internal intervention plans). A criterion that verifies extinguisher presence without verifying it is the correct class for the actual fuel/ignition risk present, or without verifying evacuation routes are actually clear, addresses only one leg of this three-part model.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** extinguisher presence, type, and accessibility; evacuation route clearance and exit signage; visible ignition sources (open flame, sparking equipment, damaged electrical wiring) near flammable/gas storage; physical segregation of incompatible hazardous substances; general housekeeping as a fuel-load proxy (accumulated combustible waste — cross-reference Chapter 2).

**What documents should be requested:** the facility's internal intervention plan (Décret 09-335, where the facility's risk tier requires one); the major-hazard risk study (Décret 06-198, for facilities storing significant flammable/chemical quantities — cross-reference Chapter 6); the building-permit-stage security note (Loi 19-02), where applicable to verify the facility's construction was reviewed by civil-protection services; extinguisher service/maintenance records; for LPG/C-related activities, the Décret 21-430 operating accreditation.

**What measurements should be taken:** none identified as legally mandated field measurements in this chapter; this is a presence/condition/documentary inspection domain, not a quantitative-threshold one (contrast with Chapter 1's pH/lab-analysis model).

**What evidence should be collected:** photographs of extinguishers (including any visible service tag/date), evacuation routes, and any observed ignition-source/flammable-storage proximity violations; copies of the internal intervention plan and risk study where applicable.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** extinguishers present, correct type for the actual hazard, evacuation routes clear and marked, no open-flame/spark sources near flammable or gas storage, internal intervention plan current where required.
- **Minor non-compliance:** extinguisher service tag expired but unit otherwise present and apparently functional; minor housekeeping fuel-load issues.
- **Major non-compliance:** extinguisher absent or wrong class for the actual hazard present; evacuation route partially obstructed; internal intervention plan missing or outdated where legally required.
- **Critical non-compliance:** active ignition source present in immediate proximity to flammable/gas storage; evacuation route fully blocked; no fire-suppression equipment at all in a facility handling flammable or compressed-gas materials.
- **Not applicable:** conditions where a specific sub-criterion (e.g., LPG-specific spark-free tooling) does not apply because the facility's activity type does not involve that hazard.

**What this means in practice:** roughly half of the SafeInspect facility library plausibly falls **outside** Loi 19-02's scope entirely (abattoir, couvoir, UPD, UAB, and most produce storage/cold room) — for these, the applicable fire-safety legal basis reverts to Décret 06-198's risk-study framework (Chapter 6) and Loi 88-07/Décret 91-05 (Chapter 5), not Loi 19-02. The other half (mechanic, car wash, retail-facing bakery/semiPharma, and conditionally marble/paint/printing/GPL) plausibly **are** ERPs and should carry the fuller Loi 19-02 obligations (operating license from the wilaya committee, security note, and — if Category 1 — prevention/intervention plans) in addition to their Décret 06-198 classified-establishment obligations, since art. 4 confirms these licenses are non-substitutable (Chapter 6).

**What remains genuinely unresolved:** art. 14 confirms ERPs are further classified into **types** (by nature of activity) and **categories** (by public headcount), with the specific type/category list and corresponding security measures set by implementing regulation — this specific list has not been located, and is needed to determine, for the facility types found to be ERPs above, which type/category they fall into and what specific measures apply. This is a distinct, narrower follow-up than the broad ERP-or-not question this session resolved.

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on the SafeInspect checklist audit (Session 4): the most common structural failure is not the complete absence of fire-safety measures but under-specified verification — extinguisher criteria in several facility types are bundled with unrelated housekeeping checks in a single pass/fail item, making it unclear which condition actually failed when the combined criterion fails, and extinguisher service currency is essentially never verified (presence is checked, the service tag date is not). A second confirmed structural gap: **no criterion anywhere in the current SafeInspect checklist addresses electrical safety** (panel condition, exposed wiring, grounding) despite electrical faults being a recognized, common fire-ignition source — this manual found no specific Algerian electrical-safety-for-fire-prevention decree during this drafting pass, meaning any new criterion in this area would need to be grounded in Loi 19-02's general framework (once its full text is reviewed) or Décret 91-05's general workplace-safety framework, not asserted against a fabricated specific standard.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** any facility handling flammable liquids, solvents, or compressed gas with only a single, unspecified-type extinguisher and no internal intervention plan warrants close review of whether it should be classified into the major-hazard risk-study tier (cross-reference Chapter 6).
- **Common misinterpretation:** Décret 09-335 (facility-level internal intervention plan) and Décret 19-59 (higher-level rescue organization plan) are easily conflated — verify which level of plan is actually being requested/reviewed.
- **Good practice:** where a facility's construction post-dates Loi 19-02's 2019 entry into force, request the building-permit-stage security note as a cross-check that civil-protection review actually occurred, rather than relying solely on the current physical inspection.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** a bundled extinguisher-plus-housekeeping criterion failing should be manually decomposed by the inspector to determine which condition actually failed, since the platform's current data model does not distinguish them (flagged in the audit as a criterion-splitting recommendation).

## 10. Decision Guidance

- An active ignition source near flammable/gas storage, or a fully blocked evacuation route, should independently drive a **critical** classification and the shortest applicable re-inspection interval, mirroring the platform's verified critical-override scoring logic — this is the one chapter where the case for an immediate, non-averaged critical override is strongest, given the life-safety stakes. **For a blocked emergency exit specifically at a facility confirmed to be an ERP, this is not just a critical safety finding but a distinct criminal offense under art. 39 (6 months–2 years imprisonment and/or 500,000–1,000,000 DA), independent of the platform's own severity scoring.**
- Extinguisher-service-currency gaps (tag expired, unit otherwise present) should be **minor**, not **major** — the equipment exists and is very likely still functional; this reflects a real but lower risk than complete absence.
- A missing internal intervention plan at a facility whose risk tier requires one (cross-reference Chapter 6's risk-study criteria) should be treated as **major**, escalating to **critical** if combined with any active ignition-source or fuel-load finding in the same inspection. **Note: the prevention/intervention-plan obligation under Loi 19-02 applies only to Category-1 ERPs and high-rise/very-high-rise buildings specifically (art. 21) — for facilities outside that narrow scope, the relevant plan obligation is Décret 09-335's internal intervention plan (Chapter 6), a different and more broadly-applicable instrument.**
- **A facility confirmed as an ERP, found non-compliant with Loi 19-02's basic requirements (operating license, exit obstruction, capacity limits) today, cannot claim the benefit of the art. 44 five-year transition period** — that window closed approximately 21 July 2024. This is the opposite situation from Chapter 6's Décret 24-196 grace period, which is still active — **the two should not be confused or treated with the same leniency in decision logic.**

## 11. Related Requirements

- **Chapter 2 (Solid & Hazardous Waste):** incompatible hazardous-waste mixing and on-site waste burning are simultaneously fire-safety and waste-management findings.
- **Chapter 6 (Documentation & Licensing):** the major-hazard risk study (Décret 06-198) and the facility's classified-establishment license tier directly determine which fire-safety obligations (internal intervention plan, risk study) actually apply — this chapter's criteria should be triggered by that classification, not applied uniformly.
- **Chapter 5 (Occupational Health, forthcoming):** Loi 88-07 and Décret 91-05 are the shared legal foundation for both workplace fire-ignition-source prevention and general occupational safety; PPE and evacuation-training obligations should be cross-referenced rather than independently re-derived in both chapters.
