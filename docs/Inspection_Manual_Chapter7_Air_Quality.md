# CHAPTER 7 — AIR QUALITY & ATMOSPHERIC EMISSIONS
## نوعية الهواء والانبعاثات الجوية

## 1. Purpose

- **Public health impact:** particulate and VOC emissions from workshop-scale activities (marble cutting, carpentry, paint/solvent use) are a chronic respiratory-health pathway for both workers (cross-reference Chapter 5) and neighboring residents.
- **Environmental impact:** direct — this chapter's subject.
- **Administrative importance:** this is the one chapter in this manual where the correct legal instrument (Décret 06-138) is clearly identified and consistently well-cited across the underlying SafeInspect checklist, but its actual numeric annex content has not been located across three separate research attempts in this project — see Section 6.
- **Safety implications:** dust/vapor extraction equipment doubles as a fire-prevention control in several activities (cross-reference Chapter 3).

**Décret 06-138 — confirmed article-level procedural content (full primary text obtained this session):**

- **Art. 3 — transitional regime for existing (pre-2006) facilities:** old industrial installations are given a **5-year** grace period (**7 years for petroleum-sector installations**, per Loi 07-05) during which a separate, more lenient "allowed value" applies instead of the standard maximum — both values are given side-by-side in the annex tables (Section 6). **This directly parallels the Décret 24-196 regularization grace period found in Chapter 6** — Algeria's regulatory pattern consistently uses time-limited transitional allowances for existing facilities rather than immediate blanket enforcement of new standards.
- **Art. 4–9 — source-level control obligations:** installations must be built/operated to avoid, prevent, or minimize emissions at the source; emissions must be captured as close to the source as possible; emission points must be minimized in number; treatment equipment must be built/operated/maintained to minimize downtime, and if downtime would cause an exceedance, the operator must reduce or halt the relevant activity; emissions must be discharged via a stack/duct designed for proper dispersion.
- **Art. 10 — declaration obligation for non-classified emitters:** any facility emitting airborne emissions that is **not** subject to the classified-establishment regulation (Décret 06-198/07-144, Chapter 6) must still supply the competent authority with: the nature and quantity of emissions, the emission point's location and height above ground and its variation over time, any other characteristic needed for assessment, and the emission-reduction measures taken. **This is a materially important, previously unidentified finding: even facilities below the classified-establishment threshold have an emissions-declaration obligation under this decree** — a gap in the current SafeInspect checklist, which only checks emissions criteria for facilities already captured as classified establishments.
- **Art. 11 — self-monitoring obligation:** operators of emission-generating installations must maintain a register recording the date and results of their own analyses, per modalities set by ministerial order (and, where relevant, a joint ministerial order with the sector-specific minister); measurements are performed at the operator's own responsibility and expense.
- **Art. 12–17 — official control procedure:** analysis results must be made available to control services; competent services perform **periodic and/or surprise** inspections to verify compliance with the annex values; control includes site inspection, on-site measurement/analysis, and sampling; the operator must explain/justify any observed exceedance and present corrective actions taken or planned; each control produces a formal minutes (محضر) recording the inspectors' identity, the emitter's identity and activity, date/time/location/conditions of the site visit and measurements, observations on the emission's appearance/color/odor and the visible condition of nearby flora/fauna, on-site measurement and analysis results, sample identification (location, time, sampling conditions), and the receiving laboratory's name. **This formal-minutes structure is directly usable as a template for the SafeInspect app's evidence-recording schema for any emissions-control finding.**
- **Art. 17 — sampling/analysis methodology:** sampling, preservation, handling, and analysis methods follow applicable **Algerian standards** (النظام الجزائري — i.e., IANOR/ANSI standards, cross-reference the Algerian Institute of Standardization) — meaning a fully rigorous implementation of this chapter's evidence standard would also need to identify the specific applicable Algerian standard numbers for sampling method, a further research item beyond this session's scope.

## 3. Scientific Background

Point-source industrial air emissions are controlled via two complementary mechanisms internationally, both conceptually present in Algerian regulation even where this manual could not extract full numeric detail: **equipment-based control** (extraction, filtration, wet suppression — verifiable by physical inspection) and **concentration-based control** (measured emission concentration compared against a numeric ceiling — verifiable only by instrumented measurement or lab analysis). The SafeInspect checklist audit found the second mechanism implemented in only one facility type (UAB); this manual's research could not yet supply the actual numeric ceiling that would let it be extended to others, which remains the chapter's central open item.

## 4. Inspection Methodology

**What the inspector should observe:** extraction/filtration/suppression equipment presence and operating condition; visible dust accumulation or odor as an indirect indicator of inadequate control; equipment bypass evidence.

**What documents should be requested:** the self-monitoring register required under art. 11 (date and results of the operator's own analyses); where the facility is not a classified establishment, the art. 10 declaration (emission nature/quantity/location/height/reduction measures) supplied to the competent authority; the Décret 07-299 pollution-tax registration as a documentary cross-check.

**What measurements should be taken:** field measurement is not the municipal inspector's role for this chapter's numeric parameters — art. 17 specifies sampling/analysis must follow applicable Algerian standards and be performed by/for the operator (art. 11) or by the competent control service during a formal inspection (art. 13–16), not read directly off an inspector's handheld instrument. The inspector's role is to verify the self-monitoring register exists and is current, and to request the formal control minutes from the competent service where a periodic/surprise control has occurred.

**What evidence should be collected:** photographs of extraction/suppression equipment; copy of the most recent emissions measurement record where one is legally required.

## 5. Compliance Criteria

- **Compliant:** appropriate extraction/suppression equipment present and operating; measurement record current and within limits, where required.
- **Minor non-compliance:** equipment present but measurement record administratively overdue.
- **Major non-compliance:** equipment present but visibly inadequate (heavy dust accumulation, visible bypass); measurement shows exceedance.
- **Critical non-compliance:** no emission-control equipment at all for a known high-emission activity (paint booth, marble cutting, uncontrolled dust-generating milling).
- **Not applicable:** activity genuinely generates no significant airborne emission (verify against the technical file).

## 8. Frequent Non-Conformities

Based on the SafeInspect checklist audit (Session 8): the dominant pattern is equipment-presence-only verification across every workshop-type facility except UAB — paint shop, printing, marble, carpentry, and blacksmith all stop at "extraction system present," with no facility type outside UAB ever comparing an actual measured value against a legal ceiling.

## 9. Inspector Guidance

- **Red flag:** visible heavy dust accumulation or persistent solvent odor despite extraction equipment being nominally present — indicates the equipment is undersized, malfunctioning, or being bypassed.
- **Common misinterpretation:** equipment presence is not equivalent to regulatory compliance — it is the necessary but not sufficient condition, pending the measurement-comparison mechanism this chapter could not fully resolve.
- **Good practice:** cross-check the Décret 07-299 pollution-tax registration — a facility disclosing itself for tax purposes as an industrial air polluter but showing no corresponding control equipment on-site is a strong inconsistency worth flagging.
- **Situation requiring additional investigation:** any high-emission activity (paint, solvent, fine particulate) with no visible control equipment at all warrants immediate escalation regardless of this chapter's unresolved numeric gap — the critical-non-compliance threshold does not depend on having the annex table in hand.

## 10. Decision Guidance

- Complete absence of control equipment for a known high-emission activity should independently drive a **critical** classification — this determination does not require the still-missing numeric table, since it's an equipment-presence question, not a measurement-comparison one.
- Equipment present but unmeasured should be **major**, reflecting genuine uncertainty rather than either compliance or critical failure.
- Once the annex table is located, exceedance findings should be recalibrated against Chapter 1's precedent (major for standard exceedance, critical for exceedance beyond any derogation/tolerance value, following the two-tier structure Décret 06-141 uses).

## 11. Related Requirements

- **Chapter 3 (Fire Safety):** dust/vapor extraction equipment is simultaneously a worker-protection, fire-prevention, and emissions-control measure — avoid re-deriving its adequacy independently in three chapters.
- **Chapter 5 (Occupational Health):** the same equipment serves worker exposure control; cross-reference rather than duplicate.
- **Chapter 6 (Documentation & Licensing):** whether a facility's category (Décret 07-144, once its list is obtained) triggers a mandatory emissions-monitoring obligation should be determined there, not independently assumed per facility type here.
